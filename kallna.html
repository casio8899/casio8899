Kallna.

Making carbon reduction a little less overwhelming.

Exploring how challenges, friendly competition, and thoughtful design could make climate action more engaging and accessible.

ClientSvalna AB
RoleUX Designer
Duration5 months
Year2022
01 / The Challenge

Making climate action feel achievable.

Reducing your carbon footprint sounds straightforward. Understanding what your emissions mean — and knowing what to do about them — is another story.

For my master's thesis, I collaborated with Svalna AB to explore how their carbon calculator could become more engaging through challenges, friendly competition, and opportunities to learn from others.

The challenge was to make climate action feel motivating without making users feel judged.

02 / Research

Understanding what motivates people.

We explored gamification, nudging, and pro-environmental behaviour through a literature review, benchmarking, a questionnaire, and ten semi-structured interviews with experts and people experienced in climate-related competitions.

Competition isn't for everyone.

Public rankings could create discomfort and discourage participation.

Learning from others matters.

Participants wanted inspiration and practical ideas from other users.

Carbon data needs clarity.

Users needed more accessible ways to understand their emissions and progress.

03 / Ideation

Exploring ways to make it engaging.

We explored different ways to encourage participation, from badges and quizzes to social challenges and notifications.

Rather than focusing only on competition, we wanted to create opportunities for people to learn, participate, and feel encouraged to take action.

04 / Wireframing

From ideas to interactions.

Through six design iterations, we explored how users could compare progress, discover challenges, and learn from one another.

Early wireframes helped us examine both the structure of the experience and the details of individual interactions.

05 / Design Decisions

Motivation without judgment.

01. A more thoughtful leaderboard

An early concept ranked all participants publicly. Research suggested this could discourage participation.

We simplified the leaderboard to highlight the top three participants while allowing users to check their own ranking privately.

02. Challenges as opportunities to learn

Challenges gave users concrete activities to try. Exploring challenges completed by others could also provide inspiration for their own actions.

03. Making emissions understandable

Visual progress indicators and categories such as Food, Transport, Household, and Shopping helped break down complex emissions data into something more approachable.

06 / Testing

Testing, learning, refining.

We conducted formative think-aloud testing with low- and high-fidelity prototypes throughout the design process.

Feedback reinforced the importance of simplifying carbon data, reducing the pressure of public comparisons, and helping users learn from other participants.

07 / Outcome

Meet Kallna.

Kallna — Swedish for "cool down" — brought together five main areas: Home, Team, Me, Leaderboard, and Challenges.

Together, these features aimed to make an abstract environmental issue feel more understandable, social, and actionable.

08 / Reflection

What I took away.

One of the most interesting lessons from this project was that competition doesn't automatically make an experience more motivating.

How people feel about being compared matters just as much as the competition itself. Kallna challenged us to balance engagement with sensitivity and make complex environmental data easier to understand.

While usability testing helped us refine the concept, we didn't investigate whether it would sustain engagement or reduce emissions over time. That would be an important next step.
