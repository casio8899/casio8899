Remind.

Reliving memories through music. Exploring how nostalgia and physical interaction can create meaningful connections with everyday objects.

ProjectStudent project
UniversityChalmers
RoleUX Designer, team project
Duration3 months

The Challenge

What makes us hold on to certain objects, even when they no longer serve a practical purpose?

Our challenge was to design a tangible artefact that could be taken apart and repaired, encouraging people to maintain it rather than replace it.

How might we design a product that people feel emotionally connected to and want to keep over time?

Understanding Emotional Attachment

Through photo journaling and follow-up interviews, we explored why people develop emotional connections with physical objects.

01 — Memories create attachment

Objects associated with personal experiences often carry emotional value beyond their practical function.

02 — Music evokes nostalgia

Music emerged as a powerful connection to memories from childhood and adolescence.

03 — Physical interaction matters

Tangible interactions can contribute to a more personal experience.

From Insights to Concept

Inspired by traditional cassette tapes, we combined digital music with the nostalgic ritual of physically rewinding a tape.

Remind lets users explore music associated with different stages of their lives. The concept includes a proposed Spotify integration and a pencil-like tool used to rewind the cassette.

Prototyping & Testing

We developed a physical prototype and conducted task-based usability testing with three participants using the think-aloud method.

Participants responded positively to the nostalgic concept and physical interaction. However, selecting a year was not immediately intuitive.

One participant interpreted the pencil-like tool as something to write with rather than turn.

Reflections & Next Steps

Remind challenged us to think beyond efficiency and explore the emotional value of physical interactions.

A key learning was that meaningful experiences require a balance between emotional engagement and usability.

Future iterations would focus on improving the year-selection mechanism and testing the concept with more users.
