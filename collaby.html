Collaby — a concept for creative collaboration at a distance.

01 / The challenge

One meeting. Too many tools.

Remote creative workshops often involve moving between video calls, shared whiteboards and different meeting rooms. Each tool solves part of the problem, but moving between them can interrupt the creative process.

For an individual project at Chalmers University of Technology, I explored how video conferencing and real-time collaboration could work together in a single desktop application.

The question was simple: How might we make remote creative collaboration feel more natural?

02 / Research & insights

Finding the friction.

I explored existing collaboration and video-conferencing tools to understand where the experience could be improved. Three challenges stood out.

Who's doing what?

Shared workspaces needed clearer ways to show who created or edited content.

Where did everyone go?

Moving between breakout rooms could make collaboration harder to follow.

People or workspace?

Video feeds and shared content competed for valuable screen space.

Research material and early exploration will be added here.

03 / The concept

Meet Collaby.

Collaby combines video calls, shared canvases and meeting management in one workspace.

The design balances a playful visual approach with practical tools for organising and creating together. Instead of treating meetings and collaborative work as separate activities, they become parts of the same experience.

Working together, visibly.

Shared canvases let participants brainstorm and develop ideas in real time. Coloured cursors and editing indicators help make individual contributions visible without interrupting the work.

Shared canvas with participant cursors and integrated video.

A different kind of video call.

The Bubbles view explores a more flexible and playful way to display meeting participants, with different layout options depending on the activity.

Bubbles view — video participants displayed within the workspace.

Making room for collaboration.

Collaby also explores how meeting rooms can be created and managed more visually, helping participants move between different activities.

From conversation to ideas.

The shared workspace supports different ways of organising thoughts and building on each other's contributions.

04 / Testing & refinement

Playful is good. Clear is better.

I tested the interactive prototype using think-aloud sessions to observe how people understood and navigated the interface.

Participants responded positively to the playful approach and the visibility of individual contributions. However, transitions between different canvases were not always intuitive.

This revealed an important area for further refinement: helping users understand where they are and how to move between workspaces.

05 / Reflection

It's not just about the features.

Collaby taught me that combining useful features doesn't automatically create a seamless experience. The connections between activities matter just as much as the activities themselves.

If I continued developing the concept, I would focus on clearer canvas navigation and smoother transitions between different types of collaborative sessions.

The final deliverables were high-fidelity interface designs and an interactive concept prototype created in Adobe XD.
